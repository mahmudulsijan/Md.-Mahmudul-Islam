Final year project

Home Appliance Monitoring and Controlling System Using NodeMCU: a low-cost IoT system that switches lights and a fan from a phone, a web page or by voice.

Submitted May 2025. Supervisor: Dr. Monir Morshed, Professor, Department of ICT, MBSTU. Team of two.

The problem

Ordinary switches need you in the room, and commercial smart-home kits can be costly and fiddly to set up. We built a low-cost alternative on an ESP8266 board and a relay module.

What it does

  • Switches two lights and a fan from a phone app or web dashboard.
  • Responds to voice commands through Alexa or Google Home.
  • Shows each appliance’s live on/off status.
  • Keeps working with wall switches if Wi-Fi drops.

My contribution

I carried out the whole project myself: designing and wiring the circuit, programming the NodeMCU, setting up Sinric Pro and voice control, and building the prototype. My teammate Kishan Kumar Das helped by collecting information for the report and slides.

How a command travels

  1. CommandTap in the app, or speak to Alexa or Google Home.
  2. CloudSinric Pro passes the on/off request to the NodeMCU over Wi-Fi.
  3. DecisionThe NodeMCU sets the matching GPIO pin high or low.
  4. SwitchingThe relay closes or opens, and the light or fan turns on or off.

Hardware

NodeMCU (ESP8266), 3-channel relay module, manual push switches, lights and a fan.

Software and services

Arduino IDE (C++), ESP8266WiFi, SinricPro and ArduinoJson libraries, Sinric Pro cloud, Alexa and Google Home.

Limits

  • Remote and voice control need Wi-Fi and the Sinric Pro cloud.
  • The free Sinric Pro plan covers three devices.
  • It is a prototype: security and device compatibility need more work.

Next steps

  • A cellular board (TTGO T-Call) for places without Wi-Fi.
  • Energy and environment sensing.
  • Adaptive automation with AI/ML from usage history.
